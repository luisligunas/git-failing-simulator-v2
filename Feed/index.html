1 · Cards

Marketing placements. No byline, no timestamp, nothing to heart — they are slots, not messages. Three of them. Feature and Notice hold one thing; Collection holds several and defines its own item shape — there is no generic container you can drop any card into, so a Feature cannot go inside a Collection, and a Collection never links out to a bigger one.

Feature card — one card, four image positions

The same record rendered four ways. Identical image, tag chip, timing chip, headline, sub-line and CTA. All that changes is where the image sits relative to the words, so the editor switches it with one dropdown and nothing is re-authored or lost. Image only is its own position rather than a state of the other three, because with every optional field switched off those three render identically — three settings producing one result is a trap, not a feature.

Media blocks

Aspect ratios

A constraint on every image and video below, not a block of its own. Media fills the card width at its own shape anywhere between 16:9 and 4:5. Wider than 16:9 is centre-cropped. Taller than 4:5 is not cropped — it is fitted whole inside a 4:5 box, so one portrait shot can never swallow the screen and nothing is ever cut off. Mosaic tiles are the one exception: with two or more images every tile is cropped to fit the grid, and the full-screen viewer is where the originals are seen whole.

Delivery — schedule, audience, order, removal, paging, refresh

None of these are properties of any component here. Every item in the catalogue — card or post — carries the first four, and they are resolved on the server before the feed is sent. The last two are how the app asks for it. The app asks for the feed and renders what comes back, a page at a time, in the order it comes back. It never evaluates a date, never evaluates a targeting rule, never sorts, and never receives an item it should not show.
Schedule
A start, and an end if the item should stop on its own. An item past its end is simply not sent. The start is also what the byline timestamp counts from, so scheduling a post early never makes it arrive looking stale.
Audience
Who the item reaches. Reuse the targeting rules Enterprise already has for missions rather than inventing a second audience model.
Position
Where the item sits. Marketing drags items into the order they want; the server sends that order and the app does not re-sort. Newest-first is a default, not a rule — a notice has to be able to sit above a week-old post.
Hidden
One switch that pulls an item out of the feed immediately, without deleting it or touching its schedule. For content that stopped being true before its end date — a cancelled raffle, a superseded notice. Flipping it back restores the item and its hearts.
Paging
The Feed scrolls without end, so it is served a page at a time. Use a cursor, not an offset — items are hidden and published while someone is mid-scroll, and an offset would then skip rows or repeat them. The cursor is a position in the server’s own order, so manual ordering and paging never fight.
Refresh
Pull down to refresh at any time. The app also watches for the connection coming back and retries on its own, so an error screen heals without anyone touching it. On returning to the Feed tab it refetches only when the last result was empty or failed — those are the two states with nothing worth keeping and no scroll position to lose. A populated feed is left alone, so coming back to the tab never throws away where someone was; pull-to-refresh is their way out of a stale one.
Why it matters for the build. Targeting rules already exist in Enterprise for missions. Reusing them means the Feed needs no audience model of its own — and it keeps rules out of the client, where a stale build would otherwise keep showing an item the rules have since excluded.

Deep links

The Feature card is the only thing in the Feed that can point anywhere inside the app. Collection items and article links are external URLs and nothing else. Where the full list does apply, it is four things and no more: a run, any tab except Feed (Missions, Submissions, Game, Wallet, Account), the daily check-in, or an external URL. A short, closed list keeps the editor a dropdown rather than a free-text field nobody can validate. Leaving it empty is a real choice — the card is then not tappable. Setting one without a CTA is also fine: the whole card becomes the tap target, with no button to say so.
Build the router with this, not after it. Every destination is a deep link, so shipping the Feed means shipping a route table that resolves a link to a screen. Push notifications need exactly the same thing when a tap has to open a specific run or tab — do it once here and notification handling inherits it, rather than growing a second implementation that drifts from the first.
